Executive Summary§ 1
The U.S. economy enters late August with demand visibly cracking even as inflation cools — a decisive turn from the energy-driven, mildly stagflationary posture of early summer. The labour market has confirmed a downshift: July payrolls fell −23K, the first outright monthly decline since 2020, with the prior two months revised down a combined −103K. Consumer spending buckled alongside — retail sales −0.6% m/m, the first drop of the run. Meanwhile disinflation is broadening: core CPI eased to +2.5% y/y, its lowest since 2021, and producer prices came in soft. Financial conditions stay accommodative — the yield curve is normally sloped and credit spreads are contained — so this reads as a demand-side soft patch, not a hard landing. The July FOMC held at 3.50–3.75% but split 9–3 with three hawkish dissents. All eyes now turn to Jackson Hole (Warsh's keynote, 29 Aug) and July PCE (26 Aug), the decisive mandate read.

What the Data Is Saying§ 3

Inflation — disinflation broadening

The summer energy spike has faded and the cooling is now broad-based. July CPI landed in line at +0.1% m/m / +3.4% y/y, with core CPI at +2.5% y/y — its lowest since 2021 and a third consecutive step down (2.9% → 2.6% → 2.5%). Producer prices confirm the pipeline is cooling: July PPI was flat (0.0% m/m) and headline PPI decelerated to +4.7% y/y from 5.5%, a miss that eases pass-through risk into August. The one live offset is ISM services prices, still elevated at 70.3. Next test — the marquee one: July PCE on 26 August, the Fed's preferred gauge.

Both consumer and producer inflation are stepping down, with core CPI now at its lowest since 2021 — the signature of durable, broadening disinflation.

Labour & growth — the turn is confirmed, demand is cracking

The soft forward signals of early summer have now pulled the hard data down. July payrolls fell −23K — the first outright decline since 2020 — with May and June revised down a combined −103K and temporary layoffs jumping +153K; the 4.1% jobless rate held only because participation slipped. Wage growth cooled to +3.2% y/y, removing the "hot wages" hedge. The one clean offset, a six-week improvement in jobless claims, has now turned: the 4-week average edged back up to 204K. Consumer demand cracked in step — retail sales −0.6% m/m, the first drop of the run, and industrial output rose just +0.2% with consumer-goods production falling. Q2 GDP grew +1.5%, but that predates the July cracks; business capex is the one leg still holding firm.

Housing — mixed and still rate-constrained

The forward and current signals diverge. July building permits rose +5.0% to 1.443M — resilient forward demand — but housing starts fell −12.4% to near post-COVID lows as multifamily gave back June's surge. With the 30-yr mortgage at 6.67%, affordability stays binding and builder sentiment is slipping.

Policy — Fed on hold but split; markets stay easy

The Fed held at 3.50–3.75% on 29 July for a fifth straight meeting, but split 9–3 with three hawkish dissents seeking a hike — the first three-way same-direction dissent since 2016. Yet with payrolls contracting and inflation cooling, the hike case has all but evaporated and an ease is now the base case; the dissent is a tone risk, not a path. Abroad, the ECB (2.25%) and BoE (3.75%) both held with a tightening bias into September. Crucially, financial conditions remain accommodative — the yield curve has fully re-steepened (10y–2y +50bp), high-yield spreads sit at a contained 275bp, and the Chicago Fed's conditions index is comfortably loose — which is why the demand crack still reads as a soft patch. The pivotal event is Jackson Hole (27–29 Aug), where Chair Warsh delivers his first major keynote.
