Executive Summary§ 1
Last week's credit-led growth scare went to the data for arbitration, and the verdict is a stand-off — the scare paused but did not resolve. The single most-watched print, initial jobless claims, fell to 197K (a 5th straight decline, 4-week average 198.0K), holding the no-fire leg intact and keeping the labour decoupling — stalled hiring, no layoffs — in place. Credit stabilised: the high-yield spread narrowed −9bp to 315bp, its first easing week since blowing through the ~290bp tripwire, though a +6bp bounce on Friday marks it a choppy retracement rather than a reversal (investment-grade −4bp to 82bp, stress not broadening). And the FOMC minutes (7 Oct) defused October — all twelve backed September's hike and "most" saw another +25bp likely by year-end, but with no urgency for the 28 Oct meeting; CME now prices October at just ~20% and December at ~85%. Against that benign read, three signals kept deteriorating. Continuing claims rebounded +17K to 1.716M off their 1.699M low, with median unemployment duration near a 4½-year high — the first fresh soft leg since the hiring turn, as the matching function, not firing, becomes the friction. The consumer darkened further: UMich sentiment fell to 46.3 (a 3rd straight monthly decline, lowest since May's record low) with the 5–10-year inflation anchor rising a second month to 3.5% and the 1-year at 4.7% — an unambiguously stagflationary mix alongside Conference Board confidence at a 12-year low. And the weekly conditions index tightened again: NFCI −0.494 / ANFCI −0.504, a second straight record-sized tightening tick, even as spreads and vol (VIX 15.4) relaxed. The counter-current remains inflation — core PCE is still 3.0% (benchmark-driven, August) versus core CPI 2.4%, with ISM Prices Paid at 77.9% (manufacturing) and 74.0% (services, a since-2022 high) and Brent above $100 threatening pass-through. Net — the Fed's base case is now a December hike, not October; the hard-landing trigger (a claims break >230K) did not arrive, and the hawkish-revival trigger is next week's September CPI.

What the Data Is Saying§ 3

Inflation — no new print; the mandate gauge sits at 3.0% as the pipeline pushes up

No fresh inflation release landed this week, so the mandate gauge still reads core PCE +3.0% y/y for August — a level reached mostly via the BEA's annual benchmark revision (July core cut 3.3%→3.0%) rather than fresh cooling, and still a full point above target. The more telling signal is the pipeline, which is pushing the other way: ISM Services Prices Paid hit 74.0% this cycle — its highest since July 2022 — alongside Manufacturing's 77.9%, dual multi-year input-cost highs, with Brent crude above $100 threatening pass-through. Core PCE (3.0%) has now run hotter than core CPI (2.4%) for ten straight months, the longest such inversion since 1983. Along the lead/lag chain, prices-paid feeds PPI and then core CPI/PCE with a one-to-three-month lag — so the mandate gauge and the pipeline point in opposite directions. The arbiter is September CPI, due ~13–15 October: a hot core would revive the October-hike case; a soft core cements the December-or-nothing path.

Labour — the no-fire leg held, but continuing claims cracked at the margin

The week's most-watched print came in benign: initial jobless claims fell to 197K (a 5th straight decline, 4-week average 198.0K, the lowest since early July), holding the no-fire leg and keeping the labour decoupling — stalled hiring, no layoffs — intact. But the first fresh soft leg since the hiring turn appeared underneath: continuing claims rebounded +17K to 1.716M off their 1.699M low, and the median duration of unemployment sits near a 4½-year high (~11.5 weeks). The signal is subtle but real — people aren't being fired, but those who lose a job are taking longer to find the next one; the matching function, not firing, is becoming the friction. Last week's hiring-leg crack is unchanged in the backdrop: September payrolls +29K against ~+85K consensus, −60K of prior revisions, JOLTS openings at a since-March low of 7.079M, and average hourly earnings decelerating to +3.0% y/y. A decoupling regime still, but with the no-fire leg beginning to wobble. The hard-landing scenario requires initial claims to break and hold above ~230K — not yet in the data.

Growth & housing — resilient activity, a deepening trade drag

The real economy is still carrying momentum. The Q2 GDP third estimate held at +2.2% annualised, with real final sales to private domestic buyers +4.6% — robust underlying demand — alongside a strong consumer (August retail control group +1.4%) and a capex boom (core capital-goods orders +1.6%). ISM Services held in expansion at 54.9 in September (a 27th month), with Employment back above 50, even as its Prices Paid sub-index climbed to 74.0%, the highest since July 2022. The soft pockets are real: industrial production was flat, and the August trade deficit widened to −$105.6B (imports surging +4.3%), a deepening Q3 net-exports drag. Housing is stale on the blackboard this week (its section was auto-restored from an archived version, data as of early October / August releases): permits −2.7%, starts −2.6% and existing sales −2.0% left the sector near cycle lows with the 30-year mortgage ~7.03%, though new-home sales had bounced +6.4% to an eight-month high on price cuts. The expansion is carried by capex, the consumer and services — not manufacturing output, trade, or the housing pipeline.

Policy, markets & sentiment — the minutes defuse October, the scare pauses

The FOMC minutes (7 Oct) were the policy event of the week: all twelve members backed September's hike and "most" judged another +25bp likely appropriate by year-end, but with no urgency for October — validating the market's fade. Post-minutes, CME prices the 28 Oct meeting at just ~20% and December at ~85%; December is now the follow-through default unless September PCE (29 Oct) forces the issue. Markets exhaled: the high-yield spread narrowed −9bp to 315bp — its first easing since the break — the front end slipped (2-year −3bp to 4.75%), the 10-year eased to 5.22% off its 5.24% cycle high, breakevens stayed anchored (5y5y 2.33%) and the VIX fell to 15.4. But the weekly conditions data diverged outright: the NFCI (−0.494) and ANFCI (−0.504) logged a second straight record-sized tightening tick even as spreads and vol relaxed — risk markets loosening while the conditions index tightens. And sentiment kept deteriorating: UMich fell to 46.3 with the long-run inflation anchor rising to 3.5%, atop a Conference Board reading at a 12-year low — stagflationary, and a 1–3-month lead on rising unemployment. Abroad, the ECB's September HICP flash jumped to +3.8%, and the BoE held hawkish. The Fed remains boxed between a wobbling labour market and 3.0% core inflation with a hot price pipeline.
