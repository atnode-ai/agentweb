Executive Summary§ 1
The U.S. data turned this week — and it turned the same way across three domains at once. July payrolls fell 23K, the first outright monthly decline since 2020, and 103K of downward revisions nearly erased May and June's gains. Days later, July retail sales dropped 0.6% — the first genuine contraction of this run — and the University of Michigan's August sentiment reading collapsed to 51.0. The price side is friendlier: core CPI plunged to +2.5% y/y, its lowest since early 2021, extending a clear disinflation even as producer prices stay firm. The Federal Reserve held at 3.50–3.75% for a fifth straight meeting — Chair Warsh's second — but with hiring cracking and core cooling, the hawkish hike thread that three dissenters pushed has gone quiet; a rate cut is now the base case. Financial conditions remain a tailwind, not a warning. The decisive read is 26 August: Q2 GDP's second estimate and, above all, July core PCE.

What the Data Is Saying§ 3

Inflation — core disinflation broadens

The marquee print is the collapse in underlying consumer inflation: core CPI fell to +2.5% y/y in July, its lowest since early 2021 and the third straight month of cooling (2.9% → 2.6% → 2.5%). Headline CPI sits at +3.3% y/y on an energy base effect. The one live offset is on the producer side — PPI held flat month-on-month but firmed to +4.7% y/y (core +4.2%), and ISM Services prices reaccelerated to 70.3 — so goods-pipeline pressure has not fully cleared. Even so, the direction of the Fed-relevant gauge is unambiguous. Next test: July core PCE on 26 August.

Labour & growth — the turn arrives

The labour market turned decisively. July payrolls fell 23K — the first outright monthly decline since 2020 — and revisions cut 103K from May and June, leaving the trailing 12-month pace at just +34K a month. The 4.1% unemployment rate flatters the picture: participation slipped and temporary layoffs jumped +153K, a classic leading signal. Wage growth cooled to +3.2% y/y, removing the "hot wages" hedge. The same week, July retail sales fell 0.6% — the first real contraction of the cycle — confirming the CARTS nowcast and lining up with the jobs turn. Growth was still solid through Q2 (GDP +1.5%, ISM manufacturing at a three-year-high 55.6), but that data predates the cracks now forming in demand.

Housing — choppy but stabilising

Activity is uneven rather than deteriorating. June permits slipped −2.6% while starts surged +19.0% on a multifamily rebound; July existing-home sales eased −1.7% to 4.06M but beat expectations and held +0.7% y/y. With the 30-yr mortgage at 6.69%, affordability is the binding constraint — but there is no sign of a sharp downturn.

Policy — a hold with a fading hawkish bias

The Fed held at 3.50–3.75% on 29 July, a fifth straight hold, on a 9–3 vote with three hawkish dissents — the first three-way same-direction dissent since 2016. But that vote predates the labour turn; with core cooling and demand cracking, the market now leans toward an ease. Abroad, the ECB hiked +25bp in June (2.25% deposit rate) and a firm July HICP keeps a September move live, while the BoE held at 3.75% with a tightening bias. Financial conditions, newly tracked in this edition, stay accommodative: both yield-curve slopes are positive, high-yield spreads are compressed at 271bp, and bank lending standards are easing.
