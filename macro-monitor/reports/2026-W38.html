Executive Summary§ 1
The Fed delivered, and the market absorbed it as a credible hawk — the September tightening wave is now complete. The FOMC hiked +25bp on Wednesday 16 September to 3.75–4.00% — a unanimous 12-0, its first hike since July 2023, and Chair Warsh's third meeting — with the statement dropping its "supply shocks" language to reframe inflation as demand-driven and a hawkish dot-plot (median 4.125% end-2026 = one more hike; no 2027 cuts; longer-run rate lifted to 3.2%). It caps a clean sweep: the ECB hiked +25bp on 10 September to a 2.50% deposit rate, and the BoE held at 3.75% on 17 September in a 6-3 vote with unusually hawkish minutes. The reaction was textbook credible-Fed absorption, not a fresh shock (the move was ~90% priced): the front end firmed but the repricing decelerated (2y +11bp to 4.67% vs last week's +22bp), the long end eased (10y −1bp to 4.94%), the curve flattened (10y–2y +27bp, still dis-inverted), and — most tellingly — breakevens fell 7bp (10y 2.33%) while real yields rose to 2.61%: lower expected inflation, higher real rates. Credit and vol confirm calm (HY spreads 270bp, VIX back sub-16 at 15.44, NFCI −0.56). The data justified the hike: August core CPI re-accelerated to +0.3% m/m (core PCE still +3.3% y/y, August print 30 Sep), the labour market showed no turn (August payrolls +162K, claims deepening to a >2-year low), and the consumer roared back (August retail sales +1.2% / control group +1.4%). The counter-current is a two-track split: manufacturing output fell for the first time in eight months (Aug IP mfg −0.3%), housing sits at cycle lows, and sentiment collapsed (UMich 47.8) as both inflation anchors rose. Net — a credible-hawkish Fed tightening into a two-track economy; the arbiters of the follow-through hike land at month-end (core PCE 30 Sep, UMich final ~25 Sep).

What the Data Is Saying§ 3

Inflation — the data justified the hike

The last inflation read before the FOMC tilted the September call decisively toward a hike. August CPI printed headline +0.4% m/m / +3.4% y/y (in line), but core +0.3% m/m — 0.1pp hotter than the +0.2% consensus and the largest core monthly gain since April — even as core year-on-year ticked to a new cycle low of 2.4%. Gasoline (+3.9%) and airfares drove the monthly re-acceleration after two disinflationary months. The August PPI (10 Sep) was softer on the pipeline — core +0.2% m/m / +4.6% y/y — so this is a consumer-core monthly-momentum story, not a producer blow-out. That was exactly the ammunition the hawkish bloc needed, and it, alongside the payrolls beat, was what the Fed cited in reframing inflation as demand-driven.

The mandate gauge is still stalled above the cooler headline

The disinflation trend on the consumer side is intact on a year-on-year basis — core CPI at a four-year low of +2.4% — but the gauge the Fed actually targets is not cooperating. Core PCE held at +3.3% y/y (July reading; the August print lands 30 Sep), failing to extend its earlier drop and sitting 1.3pp above the 2% mandate. That gap — a cooling CPI trend against a stalled PCE gauge, now with core CPI monthly momentum re-accelerating — is the crux of the follow-through debate. Core PCE on 30 Sep is the single most important print for the odds of an October or December hike.

Core CPI eased to a four-year low of 2.4% year-on-year, but core PCE — the gauge the Fed actually targets — held at 3.3%, roughly a full point higher and no longer falling; and core CPI's monthly pace re-accelerated to +0.3% in August. That divergence, plus the monthly re-acceleration, is why the Fed hiked.

Growth & housing — a two-track economy, with the goods side now cracking

The read is a sharpening two-track economy. The consumer roared back: August retail sales jumped +1.2% with the control group +1.4% (a major beat vs +0.4% consensus), a strong hand-off into Q3, and ISM Services re-accelerated to 55.4 (New Orders 60.9, a 3½-year high; Prices Paid 72.6, re-igniting). But the goods and rate-sensitive side is softening: August industrial production was flat (0.0%) with manufacturing output −0.3% — its first decline in eight months — and ISM Manufacturing cooled to 54.6. Housing remains stuck at cycle lows: August existing home sales −2.0% to 3.98M (a third straight decline, inventory the highest since Nov 2019), with permits −2.7% (1.394M) and starts −2.6% (1.275M), and the 30-yr mortgage back near 6.95% keeps affordability binding. The pillar holding the expansion up is the consumer and services — not manufacturing or housing.

Policy, markets & sentiment — the Fed delivered, the anchor slipped

All three major central banks are now in tightening mode. The Fed hiked +25bp on 16 September to 3.75–4.00% (unanimous 12-0, first since July 2023) with hawkish dots; the ECB hiked +25bp on 10 September to a 2.50% deposit rate; and the BoE held at 3.75% on 17 September (6-3) with hawkish minutes and a ~4% UK-inflation warning. Markets read the Fed as credible: the front end firmed but decelerated (2y +11bp to 4.67%), the long end eased (10y −1bp to 4.94%), the curve flattened (10y–2y +27bp), and breakevens fell 7bp (10y 2.33%) while real yields rose to 2.61%. CME FedWatch now prices Oct 28 at ~40% for another hike and December ~54%. Sentiment, though, turned outright stagflationary: UMich headline collapsed to 47.8 while both inflation anchors rose — 1-yr to 4.6% and the 5–10yr long-run anchor to 3.4%, its first uptick in four months. Financial conditions nonetheless stay accommodative (HY 270bp, VIX 15.44, NFCI −0.56), which is why the repricing reads as containment, not stress.
