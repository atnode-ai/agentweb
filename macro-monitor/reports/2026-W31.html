Executive Summary§ 1
The U.S. economy closes July with disinflation broadening while growth holds up and the Federal Reserve keeps a hawkish bias — a hold that markets are treating as a pause before a hike, not a pivot. The Fed left rates at 3.50–3.75% on 29 July for a fifth straight meeting, with three officials dissenting in favour of a hike; markets still price a September increase as near-certain. Yet the data leans the other way: June core PCE — the Fed's preferred gauge — cooled to +0.1% m/m / +3.3% y/y, its first genuine monthly deceleration in roughly eight months, and June CPI was soft (core flat, +2.6% y/y). Q2 GDP missed at +1.5% annualised, but that flatters to deceive: underlying private demand accelerated to +3.9%. The clear soft spot is jobs — payrolls badly missed at +57K — though a five-week decline in jobless claims muddies the signal. The pivotal tests come fast: July payrolls (7 Aug) and July CPI (~12 Aug).

What the Data Is Saying§ 3

Inflation — disinflation broadens, pipeline still hot

June delivered the first genuine monthly cooling in the Fed's preferred gauge: core PCE +0.1% m/m (an eight-month low) and +3.3% y/y, a tenth below consensus. June CPI corroborated — core flat on the month and +2.6% y/y, with the headline down 0.4% as energy prices reversed (gasoline −9.7%). The caveat sits upstream: the producer pipeline is still hot (PPI +5.5% y/y headline, +5.1% core), and core PCE remains 1.3pp above the 2% mandate. The confirm-or-deny is July CPI (~12 Aug) — can the cooling hold below +0.2% m/m once the energy base-effect fades?

Consumer inflation is easing as energy reverses (CPI headline +3.5%, core +2.6%), while the producer pipeline stays elevated (PPI +5.5% y/y) — the open question is pass-through to July CPI.

Labour & growth — a GDP miss that flatters to deceive; a wobbling jobs signal

Q2 GDP printed +1.5% annualised (below the ~2.1% consensus), but the miss is composition, not demand: real final sales to private domestic purchasers accelerated to +3.9% (from +1.7%), with the drag from an AI-capex import surge and an inventory drawdown. The genuine soft spot is jobs — June payrolls badly missed at +57K, crossing the sub-100K threshold that flags a labour-market turn, alongside 74K of downward revisions to prior months. Yet the counter-signal is loud: jobless claims have now improved for five straight weeks (four-week average 202,750, back below the January–March range), fully retracing the spring rise. That stand-off — a weak payrolls print against firm claims — is exactly what July's jobs report (7 Aug) must resolve.

Housing — a rate-suppressed crawl

With the 30-yr mortgage at 6.66%, affordability stays capped and existing owners locked in. June starts jumped on a misleading multifamily headline while single-family starts were flat (−0.2%) and permits fell (−2.6%) — the forward pipeline is not accelerating. New-home sales edged up (+1.6%) but 9.3 months of supply keeps builders in concession mode, and existing sales slipped (−2.4%). The sector is muddling through — neither collapsing nor recovering; permits are the tell for a genuine inflection.

Policy — hawkish holds on both sides of the Atlantic

The Fed held at 3.50–3.75% on 29 July for a fifth straight meeting, with three officials dissenting in favour of a +25bp hike; markets price a September hike as near-certain. The ECB held its 2.25% deposit rate on 24 July, but a sticky-core, firmer-growth mix (euro-area Q2 GDP +0.4% q/q, July HICP +2.9% y/y) points the same way — toward a September move. The BoE held at 3.75% on 30 July in a 6–3 vote, three members preferring a hike on energy pass-through risk. The through-line: developed-market central banks are leaning hawkish even as U.S. disinflation broadens — a tension the coming data must break.
