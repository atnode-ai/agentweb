August CPI landed with headline +0.4% m/m / +3.4% y/y (in line) but core +0.3% m/m — 0.1pp hotter than the +0.2% consensus and the largest core monthly gain since April — even as core year-on-year ticked to a new cycle low of 2.4%. Gasoline and airfares drove the monthly re-acceleration, while the Fed's mandate gauge, core PCE, is still +3.3% y/y (August print not until 30 Sep), stalled 1.3pp above target. That hot core stacked on two more hawkish inputs: the +162K August payrolls beat (with July's contraction revised away) that reversed the labour-turn thesis, and — decisively — a break in the long-run inflation anchor: UMich September-preliminary 5–10yr expectations ticked up to 3.4%, the first uptick in four months, while headline sentiment collapsed to 47.8 and 1-yr expectations jumped to 4.6% — an intensifying stagflation signal. The counter-current is the real economy: housing is rolling over (August existing sales −2.0% to a 3.98M, a third straight decline) alongside a July consumer crack, against firm business capex and re-accelerating services. Net: with all three major central banks now tightening, the hawkish leg is priced and the 16 Sep FOMC + dot-plot is the marquee event — the swing is no longer whether the Fed hikes, but whether the dots endorse a further 2026 hike path.

What the Data Is Saying§ 3

Inflation — the swing print came in hot

The last inflation read before the FOMC tilted the September call toward a hike. August CPI printed headline +0.4% m/m / +3.4% y/y (in line), but core +0.3% m/m — 0.1pp hotter than the +0.2% consensus and the largest core monthly gain since April — even as core year-on-year ticked to a new cycle low of 2.4%. Gasoline (+3.9%) and airfares (+2.7% m/m, +23.4% y/y) drove the monthly re-acceleration after two disinflationary months; shelter stayed contained at +0.3%. The August PPI (10 Sep) was softer on the pipeline — core +0.2% m/m / +4.6% y/y — so this is a consumer-core monthly-momentum story, not a producer blow-out. That was exactly the ammunition the three hawkish FOMC dissenters needed, and futures repriced hard on it.

The mandate gauge is still stalled above the cooler headline

The disinflation trend on the consumer side is intact on a year-on-year basis — core CPI at a four-year low of +2.4% — but the gauge the Fed actually targets is not cooperating. Core PCE held at +3.3% y/y (July reading; the August print lands 30 Sep), failing to extend its earlier drop and sitting 1.3pp above the 2% mandate. That gap — a cooling CPI trend against a stalled PCE gauge, now with core CPI monthly momentum re-accelerating — is the crux of the hike debate. PCE on 30 Sep is the next arbiter of whether the mandate gauge finally resumes falling or confirms the stall.

Core CPI eased to a four-year low of 2.4% year-on-year, but core PCE — the gauge the Fed actually targets — held at 3.3%, roughly a full point higher and no longer falling; and core CPI's monthly pace re-accelerated to +0.3% in August. That divergence, plus the monthly re-acceleration, is why the hike debate resolved hawkish.

Growth & housing — a two-track economy, and housing rolling over

The read is a sharpening two-track economy. Q2 GDP held at +1.5% with hot internals — private domestic demand +4.2% and core PCE revised up to +3.6% — and the surveys stayed in expansion into August, with ISM Services re-accelerating to 55.4 (New Orders 60.9, a 3½-year high; Prices Paid 72.6, re-igniting) even as ISM Manufacturing cooled to 54.6. But the household side is cracking: July retail sales fell −0.6% and industrial production managed only +0.2%. Housing is now rolling over: August existing home sales fell −2.0% to 3.98M — a third straight monthly decline and the lowest pace since June 2025 — while for-sale inventory built to multi-year highs; July starts −12.4% and new-home sales −10.5% sit near cycle lows, though forward permits held +5.0%. The 30-yr mortgage near 6.67% keeps affordability binding. The pillar holding the expansion up is business capex and services — not the consumer.

Policy, markets & sentiment — the hike gets priced, the anchor slips

The +162K August payrolls beat (July's contraction revised away) and the hot core CPI drove a sharp repricing: CME FedWatch now puts a 25bp hike on 16 Sep at ~86%, up from ~58% a week ago, with UBS forecasting Sep + Dec hikes. The front end led — 2y +22bp to 4.56%, a multi-year high — with 10y at 4.95% and real yields, not breakevens, doing the work, so the market is pricing Fed credibility rather than runaway inflation. All three major central banks are now tightening: the Fed held 3.50–3.75% on a 9–3 vote with a hawkish Jackson Hole behind it, the ECB hiked +25bp on 10 Sep to a 2.50% deposit rate, and the BoE (3.75%) meets 17 Sep with a tightening tilt. Sentiment turned outright stagflationary: UMich headline collapsed to 47.8 (a sharp miss) while both inflation anchors rose — 1-yr to 4.6% and the 5–10yr long-run anchor to 3.4%, its first uptick in four months. Financial conditions nonetheless stay accommodative — the curve is normally sloped (10y–2y +39bp), high-yield spreads sit at 270bp, and the Chicago Fed index is comfortably loose (NFCI −0.56) — which is why the repricing reads as containment, not stress, for now.
