Executive Summary§ 1
A week after the hike, the market has moved decisively beyond the Fed — this is hawkish repricing, round two. The one-more-hike dot-plot has become the floor, not the ceiling: the 10-year yield jumped +24bp on the week to 5.18%, clearing 5% for the first time this cycle, driven almost entirely by real yields (+24bp to 2.85%) with breakevens flat (10y 2.33%) — a Fed-credibility tightening, not an inflation-comp scare. The front end firmed again (2y +20bp to 4.87%), the curve bear-steepened (still dis-inverted, no regime change), and CME FedWatch now prices the 28 Oct meeting at ~70% for another hike (up from ~40% immediately post-FOMC), with year-end ~93% for at least one more. Fed speakers reinforced it — Barr said "further policy adjustments are likely to be needed," echoed by Musalem and Collins — and S&P Global flash PMIs came in hot (services 58.7, manufacturing 56.7, multi-year highs) with input costs at four-year highs on fuel and wages. The data this week kept validating the hawkish path: August durable-goods core capex +1.6% (a large beat, AI-capex boom intact) confirmed resilient business investment, and jobless claims deepened again on a clean non-holiday week (4-week average 202.25K, a third straight decline). The genuine counter-current is sentiment: the UMich September final printed 48.1 — a small beat but a third straight monthly decline to a four-month low — and, critically, both inflation anchors held elevated (1-yr 4.6%, 5–10yr 3.4%), confirming rather than softening the preliminary's hawkish signal. Stress channels still are not confirming (HY 280bp, VIX 14.21, NFCI −0.555 still loose), so the delivered hike is not yet visible in weekly financial conditions. Net — a credible-hawk Fed being front-run by a market pricing a second hike, into a two-track economy whose soft pocket (housing at cycle lows, flat factory output, collapsing sentiment) is being overridden. The arbiters land at month-end: core PCE (30 Sep) and September payrolls (2 Oct).

What the Data Is Saying§ 3

Inflation — no new print, but the pipeline is heating

No fresh CPI, PPI or PCE landed this week — the August figures (CPI/PPI released 10–11 Sep) remain the standing read: headline CPI +0.4% m/m / +3.4% y/y, core +0.3% m/m (the hottest monthly gain since April) even as core year-on-year ticked to a new cycle low of 2.4%. What moved this week was the pipeline: S&P Global flash PMIs showed input costs at four-year highs on fuel and wages, and ISM services Prices Paid is already at a four-year high of 72.6. Along the lead/lag chain, elevated prices-paid feeds PPI and then core CPI/PCE with a one-to-three-month lag — so the inflation impulse is building beneath a headline that hasn't refreshed. The single most important print for the October–December hike debate is core PCE (August), due Wednesday 30 September.

The mandate gauge is still stalled above the cooler headline

The disinflation trend on the consumer side is intact on a year-on-year basis — core CPI at a four-year low of +2.4% — but the gauge the Fed actually targets is not cooperating. Core PCE held at +3.3% y/y (July reading; the August print lands 30 Sep), failing to extend its earlier drop and sitting 1.3pp above the 2% mandate. That gap — a cooling CPI trend against a stalled PCE gauge, now with core CPI monthly momentum re-accelerating — is the crux of the follow-through debate. Core PCE on 30 Sep is the single most important print for the odds of an October or December hike.

Core CPI eased to a four-year low of 2.4% year-on-year, but core PCE — the gauge the Fed actually targets — held at 3.3%, roughly a full point higher and no longer falling; and core CPI's monthly pace re-accelerated to +0.3% in August. That divergence, plus the monthly re-acceleration, is why the Fed hiked.

Growth & housing — a two-track economy; capex and new-home sales surprise up

The two-track economy is intact, and this week the resilient track added evidence. August durable-goods core capex (nondefence ex-aircraft) rose +1.6% — a large beat versus the +0.5% consensus, with the headline flat only because of a transport drag (aircraft −4.3%) — confirming the AI-driven business-investment boom. That sits alongside a strong consumer (August retail control group +1.4%) and re-accelerating, inflationary services (ISM Services 55.4, Prices Paid 72.6). In housing, August new-home sales jumped +6.4% to 684K, an eight-month high — but on price cuts and incentives, and likely partly pull-forward ahead of higher rates. The forward and rate-sensitive indicators stay soft: industrial production flat with manufacturing output down, permits −2.7%, starts −2.6%, existing sales −2.0% (inventory the highest since Nov 2019), and the 30-yr mortgage back near 7.03%. The expansion is carried by capex, the consumer and services — not by manufacturing or the housing pipeline.

Policy, markets & sentiment — the market front-runs a second hike

The September tightening wave (Fed +25bp on 16 Sep, ECB +25bp on 10 Sep, a hawkish 6-3 BoE hold on 17 Sep) is complete; the story now is follow-through, and the market is pricing it aggressively. The long end has joined the repricing: the 10-year cleared 5% for the first time this cycle (+24bp to 5.18%), driven by real yields (+24bp to 2.85%) with breakevens flat (2.33%) — Fed-credibility tightening, not an inflation scare — while the 2-year firmed to 4.87% and the curve bear-steepened without re-inverting. CME FedWatch now prices 28 Oct at ~70% (from ~40% post-FOMC), ~93% for at least one more by year-end, reinforced by Fed speakers (Barr, Musalem, Collins) and hot flash PMIs. Sentiment confirmed its stagflation signal: the UMich September final held at 48.1 (a four-month low) with both inflation anchors elevated — 1-yr 4.6%, 5–10yr 3.4% — the final validating, not softening, the preliminary. Yet stress channels are still not confirming: HY 280bp (below the ~290bp tripwire), VIX 14.21, NFCI −0.555 still loose — the delivered hike is not yet visible in weekly financial conditions, which is why this reads as repricing, not stress.

Regime Watch

  • HAWKISHThe market has answered the follow-through question — hawkishly. CME now prices 28 Oct at ~70% (from ~40% post-FOMC) and ~93% for ≥1 more by year-end, ahead of the dots' one-more median. Confirm (Oct hike firms): core PCE (30 Sep) holds ≥3.3% and September payrolls (2 Oct) print firm. Deny (market fades it): core PCE cools toward 3.0% and a soft NFP with a claims break.
  • STAGFLATIONThe stagflation signal is confirmed, not softening: the UMich September final held at 48.1 (a four-month low) with both inflation anchors elevated (1-yr 4.6%, 5–10yr 3.4%). Watch: whether the 5–10yr anchor stays ≥3.4% and core PCE (30 Sep) holds ≥3.3%; a revert to 3.3% on the anchor would deny it.
  • DOWNSIDEHard-landing risk still far off, but the rate-sensitive pocket persists: stress channels are quiet (HY 280bp below the ~290bp tripwire, NFCI −0.555 loose, curve dis-inverted), yet housing sits at cycle lows and factory output is flat-to-soft — and the fresh hike plus a 10y through 5% will press harder. Watch: HY breaching 290bp, a curve re-inversion, or a second soft IP/manufacturing print.
