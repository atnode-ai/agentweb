Executive Summary§ 1
The U.S. economy closes August with demand cracking confirmed and disinflation broad — but with one important caveat that landed this week. The labour turn is entrenched: July payrolls fell −23K (first drop since 2020), the prior two months were revised down a combined −103K, and the last clean offset — improving jobless claims — has now reversed, with the 4-week average edging back up to 205.5K. Consumer demand cracked in step (retail sales −0.6% m/m). Yet the Fed's preferred inflation gauge stalled: July core PCE held at +3.3% y/y (unrounded ~3.0% annualised), failing to extend June's drop and leaving the three hawkish FOMC dissenters their one live argument — even as core CPI eased to +2.5% y/y, a four-year low. The Q2 GDP second estimate confirmed +1.5% but revised internals the "wrong way" for the Fed — private domestic demand +4.2%, core PCE +3.6% annualised. Financial conditions remain accommodative and, if anything, firmed (high-yield spreads tightened to 263bp; the curve is normally sloped) — so this is still a soft patch, not a hard landing. The decisive reads now: Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote (Fri 28 Aug) and the September policy round.

What the Data Is Saying§ 3

Inflation — broad cooling, but the mandate gauge stalled

The consumer-price story is still one of durable disinflation: July CPI came in line at +0.1% m/m / +3.4% y/y, with core CPI at +2.5% y/y — its lowest since early 2021 and a third straight step down (2.9% → 2.6% → 2.5%) — while July PPI was flat and its headline decelerated to +4.7% y/y. But the marquee release of the week complicated the picture: July PCE (the Fed's preferred gauge, out 26 Aug) held core at +3.3% y/y, failing to extend June's drop, with the unrounded monthly print (+0.246%, ~3.0% annualised) reading firm. Real consumer spending flatlined. That stall — 1.3pp above the 2% mandate — is the one live argument the hawkish FOMC bloc still has. Next arbiters: August CPI (mid-Sep) and PCE (30 Sep).

Core CPI has eased to a four-year low of 2.5%, but core PCE — the gauge the Fed actually targets — held at 3.3%, roughly a full point higher and no longer falling. That divergence is why the hike debate isn't quite dead.

Labour & growth — the turn is entrenched, the last offset has broken

July payrolls fell −23K — the first outright decline since 2020 — with May and June revised down a combined −103K and temporary layoffs jumping +153K; the 4.1% jobless rate held only because participation slipped, and wage growth cooled to +3.2% y/y. The one clean counter-signal, a six-week improvement in jobless claims, has now decisively reversed: the 4-week average rose a second straight week to 205.5K. Consumer demand cracked alongside — retail sales −0.6% m/m and industrial output up just +0.2%. The Q2 GDP second estimate confirmed +1.5% but revised the internals hotter — private domestic demand +4.2%, core PCE +3.6% annualised, corporate profits +9.1% — a Q2 snapshot that predates the July cracks. Business capex (durable goods +1.1%; core capex +0.2%, prior month revised up) remains the one firm pillar.

Housing — mixed and still rate-constrained

Forward and current signals keep diverging. July building permits rose +5.0% to 1.443M — resilient forward demand — but housing starts fell −12.4% to near post-COVID lows and new-home sales dropped −10.5% to a six-month low, with months' supply climbing to 9.6. With the 30-yr mortgage at 6.66%, affordability stays binding and builder sentiment is slipping.

Policy & sentiment — Fed split, anchor holds, markets stay easy

The Fed held at 3.50–3.75% for a fifth straight meeting but split 9–3, with three hawkish dissents seeking a hike. Consumer sentiment is soft but not unanchored: UMich August final 51.7 saw the 1-yr inflation expectation revised down to 4.0% and the 5–10yr anchor held at 3.3%; Germany's Ifo rose a fourth straight month. Abroad, the ECB (2.25%) and BoE (3.75%) both hold with a tightening bias into a live September round (ECB 10 Sep, BoE and FOMC mid-Sep with fresh projections). Crucially, financial conditions remain accommodative and firmed this week — the curve is normally sloped (10y–2y +47bp), high-yield spreads tightened to 263bp, and the Chicago Fed index sits comfortably loose — which is why the demand crack still reads as a soft patch. The pivotal event: Jackson Hole, where Chair Warsh delivers his first keynote (Fri 28 Aug).
