Executive Summary§ 1
The labour-market turn that dominated August's narrative substantially reversed this week. August payrolls came in at +162K — the strongest month since March and a massive beat against a ~+53K consensus — while July's "first contraction since 2020" was revised from −23K to +21K and June was revised up too, a combined +55K swing that erases the outright decline. The two mechanical caveats that had flattered July unwound: labour-force participation rebounded to 61.6% so the steady 4.1% jobless rate now sits on a firmer base, and the temporary-layoff spike reversed (−82K). Critically, wages cooled to +3.1% y/y — a strong jobs print without a wage flare, the equity-friendlier mix. Inflation was unchanged on the week (no new print): core PCE still +3.3% y/y, the Fed's gauge stalled 1.3pp above target, against a cooler core CPI of +2.5%. The residual soft signals are now narrow — jobless claims' 4-week average has risen three straight weeks to 207.25K, and JOLTS shows a frozen matching function (hires at a multi-month low, quits at a post-pandemic low) — a "slow-hire, no-fire" stalemate rather than a turn. Financial conditions remain accommodative (high-yield spreads 265bp, curve normally sloped). Net: the hard-landing risk receded and the hawkish leg is back in play into the 16 Sep FOMC — with August CPI (11 Sep) the swing print.

What the Data Is Saying§ 3

Labour — the turn largely reverses

The decisive labour-turn call from a month ago is now substantially undone. August payrolls rose +162K — the strongest monthly gain since March and a massive beat against a ~+53K consensus — with gains broad-based (restaurants and bars +59K, government education +42K, manufacturing +16K). Crucially, July's −23K "first contraction since 2020" was revised up to +21K and June was lifted too, a combined +55K upward revision that removes the outright decline. Three of the four "labour-turn legs" have reversed: the negative print, the downward revisions, and the +153K temporary-layoff spike (which unwound −82K in August). Participation rebounded to 61.6%, so the steady 4.1% jobless rate now rests on a firmer base, and U-6 fell to 7.7%. Wages cooled to +3.1% y/y — a strong jobs report without a wage flare. Two soft signals remain: jobless claims' 4-week average has risen three straight weeks to 207.25K, and JOLTS shows a frozen matching function (July hires at a multi-month low, quits at a post-pandemic 1.9%). ADP's +38K — a third sub-50K miss — now reads as a clear false alarm against the +162K official count.

Inflation — broad cooling, but the mandate gauge is still stalled

No new inflation data landed this week, so the July picture stands: durable disinflation on the consumer side — core CPI at +2.5% y/y, its lowest since early 2021, with July PPI decelerating to +4.7% y/y — but the Fed's preferred gauge stuck. July core PCE held at +3.3% y/y (unrounded ~3.0% annualised), failing to extend June's drop and sitting 1.3pp above the 2% mandate. That stall is the single live argument the three hawkish FOMC dissenters retain, and with wages now cooling it is the swing variable into the September meeting. The next arbiter lands imminently: August CPI on 11 Sep, with PPI alongside and PCE on 30 Sep.

Core CPI has eased to a four-year low of 2.5%, but core PCE — the gauge the Fed actually targets — held at 3.3%, roughly a full point higher and no longer falling. That divergence is why the hike debate isn't quite dead.

Growth & housing — a two-track economy, and rate-constrained building

The Growth & Activity collector is live again (its heartbeat resumed 5 September after a mid-week gap), and the read is a two-track economy. Q2 GDP held at +1.5% with hot internals — private domestic demand +4.2% and core PCE revised up to +3.6% — and the factory and services surveys stayed in expansion into August (ISM Manufacturing 54.6, Services 55.4). But the consumer side is cracking: July retail sales fell −0.6% and industrial production managed only +0.2%. Business investment (capex) remains the firm pillar. Housing remains mixed and rate-constrained: July building permits rose +5.0% to 1.443M (resilient forward demand), but starts fell −12.4% to near post-COVID lows and new-home sales dropped −10.5% to a six-month low, with months' supply at 9.6. The 30-yr mortgage at 6.71% keeps affordability binding.

Policy & sentiment — Fed split, anchor holds, markets stay easy

The Fed held at 3.50–3.75% for a fifth straight meeting on a 9–3 vote, and Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote struck a hawkish tone — futures now price a September hike near 60%. The strong payrolls print plus cooling wages leaves the September call genuinely two-sided. Consumer sentiment is soft but anchored: UMich August final 51.7, with the 1-yr inflation expectation revised down to 4.0% and the 5–10yr anchor holding 3.3%; Germany's Ifo rose a fourth straight month. Abroad, the ECB (2.25%) meets 10 Sep with markets pricing a move to 2.50%, and the BoE (3.75%) holds with a tightening bias. Financial conditions stay accommodative — the curve is normally sloped (10y–2y +43bp), high-yield spreads sit at 265bp, and the Chicago Fed index remains comfortably loose (NFCI −0.56) — which is why the strong jobs print reads as re-acceleration, not overheating stress.
