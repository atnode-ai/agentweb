Executive Summary§ 1
The regime pivoted hard this week. A week ago the market was front-running a second Warsh hike; two data shocks have killed that call and replaced it with a dual-mandate dilemma. September payrolls collapsed to +29K against a ~+85K consensus, with −60K of downward revisions (July back to an outright loss, August cut to +133K), the 3-month average down to ~+50K and average hourly earnings decelerating to +3.0% y/y — no wage flare. Days earlier, core PCE (August) rolled to +3.0% y/y — 0.1pp cooler than consensus on all four readings, but largely via the BEA's annual benchmark revision (July core cut 3.3%→3.0%) rather than fresh cooling; real spending (+0.6%) and a falling savings rate show demand still firm. Together they met the prior week's explicit deny condition, and CME now prices the 28 Oct meeting at just ~35% for another hike (hold ~65%). But the loudest signal came from credit: the high-yield spread blew +44bp through its ~290bp tripwire to 324bp and kept widening — the first genuine distress signal of the cycle — while the front end eased for the first time this hawkish turn (2y −9bp to 4.78%) and the curve bull-steepened; the 10-year rose to a new cycle high of 5.24%, but on real yields with breakevens anchored (5y5y 2.36%). This is the market pricing growth damage, not inflation. The labour turn is confirmed on the hiring leg (NFP, plus JOLTS openings 7.079M, the lowest since March) but not on the no-fire leg: initial claims fell to 197K (4th straight decline), continuing claims 1.701M near cycle lows, Challenger layoffs −18%. Sentiment corroborated the turn — Conference Board confidence crashed −6.7 to 81.9, the lowest since 2014. The counter-current is inflation: ISM Manufacturing Prices Paid surged to 77.9% with services input costs at four-year highs. Net — the Fed is boxed between a cracking hiring leg (and a firing credit tripwire) and 3.0% core inflation; the market has chosen its side, fading the hike and pricing a growth scare. The missing confirmation for a true hard landing is a claims break.

What the Data Is Saying§ 3

Inflation — the mandate gauge rolled to 3.0%, but via revisions, not cooling

The Fed's preferred gauge finally has a 3-handle: core PCE for August printed +3.0% y/y (headline +3.4%), 0.1pp below consensus on all four readings. The relief is real but hollow — it came mostly from the BEA's annual benchmark revision, which cut July core from 3.3% to 3.0%; August held those revised levels rather than extending the drop. Beneath it, demand is still firm (real PCE +0.6%, savings rate down to 4.1%), and the pipeline is pushing the other way: ISM Manufacturing Prices Paid surged to 77.9% (+6.8pp, a 24th straight month of rising input costs) and services input costs sit at four-year highs, with Brent crude above $100 threatening October pass-through. Along the lead/lag chain, prices-paid feeds PPI and then core CPI/PCE with a one-to-three-month lag — so the headline relief and the pipeline pressure point in opposite directions. The next genuine read is September CPI, due ~13–15 October.

Labour — the hiring leg cracks, but no layoff wave

September payrolls settled the question the market had been debating: the labour turn is back on the hiring leg. NFP collapsed to +29K against a ~+85K consensus, and −60K of downward revisions erased the August "+162K false dawn" — July is back to an outright loss (−10K) and the 3-month average is down to ~+50K. The 4.2% unemployment rate rose on a benign mechanism (participation +0.2pp to 61.8%), and average hourly earnings decelerated to +3.0% y/y — no wage leverage. But the no-fire leg is intact and contradicting the payroll weakness outright: initial claims fell to 197K (a 4th straight decline, 4-week average 200.0K), continuing claims 1.701M near 2025 lows, and Challenger layoffs dropped −18%. JOLTS deepened the stalemate — openings fell to 7.079M, the lowest since March, with the quits rate at a cycle-low 1.9%. A decoupling regime: stalled hiring, dead churn, no layoffs. The hard-landing scenario still requires the no-fire leg to crack.

Growth & housing — resilient activity, a surging price impulse

The real economy is still carrying momentum. The Q2 GDP third estimate held at +2.2% annualised, with real final sales to private domestic buyers +4.6% — robust underlying demand — alongside a strong consumer (August retail control group +1.4%) and a capex boom (core capital-goods orders +1.6%). ISM Manufacturing held at 54.5 in September, with New Orders firming (+1.6pp to 55.3) and backlogs building, even as the Prices Paid sub-index surged to 77.9% near a cycle high. The soft pocket is real but contained: industrial production was flat, the trade deficit widened, and housing stayed stuck near cycle lows — August permits −2.7%, starts −2.6%, existing sales −2.0% (inventory the highest since 2019) with the 30-year mortgage back near 7.03%. New-home sales were the exception, jumping +6.4% to an eight-month high, but on price cuts and incentives and likely partly pull-forward. The expansion is carried by capex, the consumer and services — not manufacturing output or the housing pipeline.

Policy, markets & sentiment — the credit tripwire fires, the hike fades

The September tightening wave (Fed +25bp 16 Sep, ECB +25bp 10 Sep, a hawkish 6-3 BoE hold 17 Sep) is complete, and this week the follow-through reversed. The front end eased for the first time this hawkish turn (2-year −9bp to 4.78%, 3-month −7bp to 4.17%) as the soft payrolls and cooler PCE trimmed CME's 28 Oct hike odds to ~35% (from ~70%). The 10-year still pushed to a new cycle high of 5.24%, but on real yields with breakevens anchored (5y5y 2.36%), and the curve bull-steepened — the signature of markets pricing growth damage, not inflation. The decisive move was in credit: the high-yield spread blew +44bp through its ~290bp tripwire to 324bp and kept widening (+12bp on 1 Oct alone) — the loudest signal in the markets section's logged history — with the ANFCI's weekly tightening tick the largest on record, though conditions remain loose overall (NFCI −0.548, VIX 16.4, IG only +7bp). Sentiment corroborated the turn: Conference Board confidence crashed −6.7 to 81.9, the lowest since 2014, with the jobs-differential re-worsening — a 1–3-month lead on rising unemployment. The Fed is now boxed between a cracking hiring leg plus a firing credit tripwire and 3.0% core inflation with a surging price impulse.
