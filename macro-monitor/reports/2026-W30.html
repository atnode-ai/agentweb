Executive Summary§ 1
The U.S. economy heads into the last week of July with the spring energy shock fully unwound and disinflation firmly back in control. June sealed a dual dovish surprise — core CPI was flat on the month (0.0% m/m), annual core eased to +2.6% y/y, and producer prices actually fell (PPI −0.3% m/m). The labour market looks like a soft patch rather than a hard landing: June payrolls badly missed at +57K with −74K of prior-month revisions, yet weekly jobless claims have now fully reversed their spring drift to 187K — the fewest in months. Growth is still expanding (June retail control-group sales +0.5%; both ISM surveys above 50), consumer sentiment is rebounding (UMich 54.4) and long-run inflation expectations are anchored at 3.3%. The twist is in the market: the front-end has repriced to roughly a one-in-three chance of a July rate hike, pricing Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish reaction function against data that argue for easing. That pricing-versus-fundamentals gap makes the 28–29 July FOMC the pivotal event of the summer.

What the Data Is Saying§ 3

Inflation — the energy shock fully reverses

June delivered a clean dovish surprise on both price gauges. Core CPI was flat (0.0% m/m) — the first flat reading since 2023 — pulling annual core down to +2.6% y/y, its closest to the Fed's 2% target in years, with shelter posting its smallest monthly gain since 2021. Producer prices fell −0.3% m/m as final-demand goods deflated −1.4%. The March–June oil spike (Iran / Strait of Hormuz) has now washed completely out of both series; headline CPI (+3.5% y/y) and PPI (+5.5% y/y) stay elevated only on base effects, while core PPI eased to about +4.7%. The last sticky reading is core PCE at 3.4% y/y (May); the June PCE print — due this week (~29–31 July) — is the marquee test, landing alongside the Fed decision.

Labour & growth — a hiring turn, but claims push back

The monthly labour data turned in June: payrolls rose just +57K — a clear miss below the ~110K consensus and under the sub-100K "turn" threshold — with a combined −74K of downward revisions and leisure & hospitality shedding −61K. The unemployment rate ticked down to 4.2%, but only because participation fell. Yet the higher-frequency counter-signal has strengthened decisively: initial jobless claims fell to 187K — the fewest in months and a fourth straight improvement, pulling the four-week average back to 207.5K, squarely inside its early-2026 range. The spring drift higher has fully reversed, so for now the claims data say "soft patch," not "hard landing." Wages held at +3.5% y/y even as hiring cooled — a margin-squeeze mix. Growth itself is holding: June retail-sales control group rose +0.5%, both ISM surveys stayed expansionary (manufacturing 53.3, services 54.0), though industrial production was flat for a second month and the Atlanta Fed's Q2 GDPNow sits at just +1.3% annualised on trade and output drags. The Q2 GDP advance (~30 July) and June durable goods will fill in the growth picture.

Housing — soft beneath a noisy headline

The week's fresh print offered a demand-side green shoot: June new-home sales rose to 628K (a +21K beat), months' supply eased to 9.3 from 10.3, and May was revised sharply higher to 618K. But the supply side stayed weak — single-family starts fell for a third straight month (895K, the lowest since January) and permits slipped −3.0% for a second decline, while existing-home sales dipped to 4.09M (−2.4%). With the 30-year mortgage stuck near 6.58%, rate lock-in remains the structural constraint: the sector is stabilising on the demand side but its residential-construction pipeline is still a mild drag on GDP.

Policy & markets — a hawkish repricing meets dovish data

The Fed (chaired by Kevin Warsh) sits at 3.50–3.75% ahead of its 28–29 July meeting, and this is where the week's real tension lies. The incoming data argue for easing — cooling inflation, softening labour leads — but the market has moved the other way: the front-end has repriced to price roughly a one-in-three chance of a July hike (CME futures ~64% hold / ~35% +25bp, up from ~83% / ~0% a week ago), pushing the 2-year yield +18bp on the week to 4.31% and the 10-year +12bp to 4.67%. The driver is Warsh's hawkish reaction function, not the prints. Crucially, that repricing has not spread beyond the front end: broader financial conditions stay loose (Chicago Fed NFCI −0.55), high-yield spreads sit near cycle tights at 268bp and the VIX is calm at 16.6 — no stress signal. The FOMC verdict — validate the hawkish pricing, or open the September-cut door the data support — is the summer's pivot.
